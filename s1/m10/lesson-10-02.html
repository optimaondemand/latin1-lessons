Lesson 10.02
★ One Stem, a Shifting Vowel
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you learned to spot the family. Today it learns to speak — with one twist the first two families didn't have.
In the first and second conjugations, a steady long vowel (ā or ē) held every form together. The third conjugation is different: it uses a short connecting vowel that shifts — an i in most forms, a u in the last one, and nothing at all in the first. Once you hear the rhythm — agō, agis, agit, agimus, agitis, agunt — it locks in fast. Today you learn that rhythm.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…

2
I will be able to build the full present tense with the endings -ō, -is, -it, -imus, -itis, -unt.
3
I will be able to explain why the connecting vowel is i in most forms but u in the third person plural.

📚 PART 2 · FIND THE STEM 8 min

Step one: get the stem

Same first move as always: start from the infinitive and drop the -ere. What's left is the present stem — and here it ends in a plain consonant, with no vowel of its own.

InfinitiveDrop -erePresent stem
agereagereag-
dūceredūceredūc-
scrīberescrīberescrīb-
💡 HOW THIS DIFFERS FROM THE FIRST TWO

In the first conjugation the stem kept a long ā (amā-); in the second it kept a long ē (vidē-). The third-conjugation stem keeps no vowel — just ag-, dūc-, scrīb-. Because the stem has no vowel to lean on, Latin supplies a small connecting vowel when it adds the endings. That connecting vowel is the whole personality of this family.

✅ PART 3 · ADD THE ENDINGS 20 min

Step two: the six endings

Here is the model verb agere (to do, drive), fully conjugated in the present tense. Read it as a chant, top to bottom:

SingularPlural
1st personagō — I do/driveagimus — we do
2nd personagis — you doagitis — you all do
3rd personagit — he/she/it doesagunt — they do
💡 THE SHIFTING VOWEL — WATCH IT MOVE

Look at the vowel sitting between the stem and the ending. In agis, agit, agimus, agitis it is a short i. In the third person plural it jumps to u: agunt (not “agint”). And in the first person singular it vanishes entirely — just stem ag- + -ō → agō. So the pattern is: nothing, then i four times, then u at the end.

Written as endings, the third conjugation uses this fixed set for every verb in the family:

EndingPerson & numberEnglish subject
-ō1st singularI
-is2nd singularyou
-it3rd singularhe / she / it
-imus1st pluralwe
-itis2nd pluralyou all
-unt3rd pluralthey

Now put the pattern to work — five reps, building forms and reading endings both directions. Keep the chart in view if you need it; the goal is to make the endings automatic.

📌 PART 4 · THREE FAMILIES SIDE BY SIDE 8 min

The endings still tell you the subject

The good news: just like the first two families, the ending carries the subject — so a single third-conjugation word is a full sentence. Here are all three families lined up so you can see how they rhyme and where they differ.

1st (amāre)2nd (vidēre)3rd (agere)
Iamōvideōagō
youamāsvidēsagis
he/she/itamatvidetagit
weamāmusvidēmusagimus
you allamātisvidētisagitis
theyamantvidentagunt

Notice the personal endings are almost the same everywhere — -ō, -s, -t, -mus, -tis, -nt are hiding in all three. What changes is the vowel in front of them. The third family is the odd one out: its short i/u replaces the steady ā and ē of the others.
