★ Five Vowels, Two Lengths — and the Little Line That Matters
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Latin has only five vowel letters — but each one hides two sounds, and one tiny mark tells you which.
In English, vowels are chaos: the “a” in cat, car, cake, and call are all different. Latin is refreshingly honest. Every vowel has just two settings — long and short — and the sound stays steady. The mark that flags a long vowel is the macron, the little bar you see over ā, ē, ī, ō, ū. It's not decoration: it can change a word's meaning and decide where you put the stress. Learn to read it and you'll pronounce Latin the way the Romans did.

📚 PART 2 · THE FIVE VOWELS 12 min

Each vowel, long and short

A long vowel is simply held about twice as long as a short one, and it usually keeps a pure, clean quality. Since there is no audio here, learn each sound by its English comparison word — say the English word aloud and hold onto that vowel.

VowelLong — as in…Short — as in…
a / āā like the a in father (māter)a like the a in aha, clipped (aqua)
e / ēē like the ey in they (tē)e like the e in pet (et)
i / īī like the i in machine (īnsula)i like the i in pin (in)
o / ōō like the o in holy (Rōma)o like the o in off (bonus)
u / ūū like the oo in rude (lūna)u like the u in put (sub)

Notice how tidy that is: five letters, ten sounds, and each one steady. There is no silent e, no “magic” vowels, no exceptions to memorize. What you see, you say.

📌 PART 3 · THE MACRON 8 min

Why the little line earns its keep

The bar over a vowel — ā, ē, ī, ō, ū — is called a macron (from Greek makros, “long”). It marks the vowel as long. A vowel with no macron is short. The Romans didn't usually write macrons, but textbooks add them so you can learn the correct length — and length genuinely matters for two reasons:

1 · It can change the meaning

Two words can look identical except for one macron and mean completely different things:

malus = bad, evil
mālus = apple tree

liber = book
līber = free

2 · It decides the stress

Whether a vowel is long or short helps determine which syllable you emphasize when you say the word. That's the whole story of Lesson 0.04 — so reading macrons now sets you up for the stress rule later.
🌱 A WORD ABOUT MACRONS

You've met the word “macron” before without knowing it: the same Greek root makros gives English macroeconomics (the “large” view) and a macro lens. In Latin, the macron is your signal to hold the vowel long.
