Lesson 8.02
★ The Ablative Endings — and the Whole Paradigm at Last
Lesson · about 42 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Two small endings finish the noun — and today you finally see all five cases together.
The ablative adds just two new endings to learn: -ā for the first declension singular and -ō for the second, with a shared -īs in the plural. That's it. And once you have them, something big happens: the paradigm is complete. For the first time you can lay out a Latin noun in all five cases, singular and plural, with nothing missing.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to form the ablative singular and plural for first- and second-declension nouns.
2
I will be able to recite the complete five-case paradigm for a first- and a second-declension noun.

📚 PART 2 · THE NEW ENDINGS 10 min

Just two to learn

Here are the ablative endings for the two declensions you know. Notice how few there really are:

1st declension2nd declension
Ablative singular-ā — terrā-ō — servō, oppidō
Ablative plural-īs — terrīs-īs — servīs, oppidīs

Two singulars, one shared plural. The long marks matter: terrā (ablative, long ā) is not the same word as terra (nominative, short a). In careful Latin the macron is the difference between “the land” and “in/with the land.”

💡 A LOOK-ALIKE TO EXPECT

The ablative plural -īs looks exactly like the dative plural -īs you learned in Module 07 — servīs can be “to/for the slaves” (dative) or “by/with the slaves” (ablative). Don't panic: the rest of the sentence — especially any preposition — tells you which it is.
