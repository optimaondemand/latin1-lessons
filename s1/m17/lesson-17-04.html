⏰ PART 1 · READ THE WHOLE STORY 12 min

Aeneas, from Troy to Rome — in Latin you can read

Six connected sentences trace the whole journey. Read each aloud, find the subject and verb, then check the gray parse line. A few small connecting words (sed = but, nunc = now, ad = to/toward) are glossed for you.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Before you begin: when you meet a connected Latin sentence, what do you reach for first?
Aenēās nauta Trōiānus fuit.

Aeneas was a Trojan sailor. — Aenēās nom. subject · nauta Trōiānus predicate nouns (adj. agrees) · fuit perfect of esse, “was.”

Iūnō dea Trōiānōs nōn amat.

Juno the goddess does not love the Trojans. — Iūnō dea nom. · Trōiānōs acc. pl. (object) · amat present 3rd sg.

Sed deī Aenēam ad Ītaliam dūxērunt.

But the gods led Aeneas to Italy. — deī nom. pl. · Aenēam acc. (object) · ad Ītaliam acc. after ad · dūxērunt perfect 3rd pl. of dūcere.

Dīdō rēgīna Aenēam amāvit.

Dido the queen loved Aeneas. — Dīdō rēgīna nom. · Aenēam acc. · amāvit perfect 3rd sg., “loved.”

Sed fātum Aenēam vocāvit.

But fate called Aeneas. — fātum nom. neut. (subject) · Aenēam acc. · vocāvit perfect 3rd sg. of vocāre.

Nunc Rōmānī Aenēam laudant.

Now the Romans praise Aeneas. — Rōmānī nom. pl. · Aenēam acc. · laudant present 3rd pl. of laudāre (to praise).

💡 NOTICE THE TENSE SHIFT

The middle sentences are perfect (dūxērunt, amāvit, vocāvit) — the completed past of the story. The last one turns to the present (laudant): what Rome still does today. Reading is partly hearing that shift in time.

Now translate on your own, from scratch: Aenēās nauta Trōiānus fuit. Write your English, then reveal.

“Aeneas was a Trojan sailor.” fuit is the perfect of esse (“was”); nauta and the adjective Trōiānus are both predicate nominatives, describing Aeneas after the linking verb.
🏛️ PART 3 · VR MUSEUM — FINAL ASSEMBLY 13 min

Turn the semester into five rooms

🏛️ YOUR MUSEUM OF AENEAS · AENEID, BOOKS 1–6

All quarter you have been planning a five-room virtual museum built around Aeneas and his journey from Troy to Rome. Now you assemble it. Each room takes one stage of the story, so the visitor walks the whole arc — the same arc you just read in Latin.

A clean five-room plan follows the Aeneid from the fall of Troy to the threshold of Rome:

⭐ FINAL-ASSEMBLY CHECKLIST
□ All five rooms are built and placed in order.
□ Each room has a title and a short description of what it shows.
□ At least two rooms carry a Latin caption — a real sentence or phrase you can parse, with correct macrons.
□ Your captions use only grammar from this semester (e.g. Dīdō Aenēam amat, Aenēās ad Ītaliam nāvigat).
□ Each room is culturally accurate to its book of the Aeneid.

Write your captions today while the grammar is fresh. A caption like Rēgīna Aenēam amat (“The queen loves Aeneas”) is more than decoration — it shows the grading team you can produce correct Latin, not just recognize it. You will submit the finished museum tomorrow alongside the Final.
