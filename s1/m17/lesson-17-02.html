📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday: the nouns. Today: the engines that make them move — your whole verb system, present and past.
You began the semester able only to say a subject is something. You end it able to make anyone act — in three conjugations, in two tenses. Today we lay the verb system out in one place: the present across the 1st, 2nd, and 3rd conjugations, the perfect for completed past action, and the one verb that refuses every pattern, esse. No new forms — just a clear look at all of them together.

📚 PART 2 · THE PRESENT, THREE FAMILIES 10 min

Same six endings, three vowels

All three conjugations use the same personal endings — -ō, -s, -t, -mus, -tis, -nt. What changes is the vowel that connects the stem to the ending. Here is one model verb from each family, in the present tense.

1st · amāre (love)2nd · vidēre (see)3rd · dūcere (lead)

💡 WATCH THE 3RD CONJUGATION

The 3rd conjugation has no long stem vowel of its own, so it borrows a short one: -i- in most forms, but -u- in “they” (dūcunt, not dūcint). That single quirk is what makes it the family to double-check.

📌 PART 3 · THE PERFECT TENSE 10 min

One set of endings for “did / has done”

The perfect tense reports a completed action — “carried,” “has led,” “said.” The beauty of it: every verb, in every conjugation, takes the same perfect endings. You find the perfect stem (the third principal part) and add them.

EndingMeaningportāre → stem portāv-
-īI …edportāvī — I carried
-istīyou …edportāvistī
-ithe/she/it …edportāvit
-imuswe …edportāvimus
-istisyou all …edportāvistis
-ēruntthey …edportāvērunt

The endings never change; only the perfect stem does, and you learn it from the verb's principal parts: habeō → habuī, dīcō → dīxī, scrībō → scrīpsī, dūcō → dūxī.

⚖️ AND THE ONE THAT BREAKS EVERY RULE — ESSE

Present: sum, es, est, sumus, estis, sunt (“I am… they are”). Perfect: fuī, fuistī, fuit, fuimus, fuistis, fuērunt (“I was… they were”). The perfect uses the fu- root but takes the same familiar perfect endings — so even the irregular verb bends to the pattern in the past.
