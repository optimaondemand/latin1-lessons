📚 PART 2 · ADJECTIVE AGREEMENT 10 min

An adjective wears its noun's endings

A 2-1-2 adjective like magnus, magna, magnum (“big”) has three sets of endings — masculine (2nd decl.), feminine (1st decl.), neuter (2nd decl.). It must agree with its noun in three ways at once: gender, number, and case.

NounGender“big” agrees asPhrase
rēx (king)masculinemagnusmagnus rēx
via (road)femininemagnamagna via
templum (temple)neutermagnummagnum templum
💡 AGREE, DON'T MATCH THE SPELLING

Agreement is about gender, number, and case — not about the letters looking identical. With a 3rd-declension noun the endings differ: magnus rēx in the nominative, but magnum rēgem in the accusative. The adjective takes its own ending for that case, matching the noun's job, not its spelling.

📌 PART 3 · PRONOUNS, COMMANDS & ADDRESS 12 min

Standing in, pointing out, calling out

Personal pronouns replace a noun for the speaker and listener, and is/ea/id points to a third person (“he / she / it”) — you learned it in the nominative and accusative this semester.

Nominative (subject)Accusative (object)
I / meegomē
you (sg.)tūtē
he / himiseum
she / hereaeam
itidid

The imperative gives a command; the vocative names the person you address. Together they are the voice of the orator.

ToolSingularPlural
Imperative, 1st conj. (portāre)portā — carry!portāte — carry! (you all)
Imperative, 3rd conj. (mittere)mitte — send!mittite — send!
Negative commandnōlī timēre — do not fear!nōlīte timēre
Vocative (2nd decl. -us → -e)Marce! — O Marcus!dominī! — O masters!

A negative command uses nōlī (“be unwilling”) + the infinitive — literally “be unwilling to fear.” That infinitive is the one place the imperative meets the “to ___” form.
