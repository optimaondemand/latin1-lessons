🌱 THE ETYMOLOGY THREAD, BOTH QUARTERS

From Q1's journey words (aqua → aquatic, portāre → transport) to Q2's language of power (dīcere → diction, vōx → vocal, imperāre → emperor), one truth has held all year: a huge share of English is Latin in disguise. That is the lasting legacy — learn Latin roots once, and you keep unlocking English for life.

✅ TRY IT · command
Tell one person to “send!” — the singular imperative of mittere is:

Now build one yourself: write a full Latin sentence with a subject (nominative), a direct object (accusative), and a present-tense verb. Say it, check the agreement, then reveal a model.

One good model: Rēx urbem tenet. — rēx (nom. subject) + urbem (acc. object) + tenet (3rd sg. present of tenēre, “holds”). Any correct subject–object–verb sentence works — just be sure the verb agrees with the subject in number.
