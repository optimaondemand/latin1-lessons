★ The Fourth Piece — Meet the Principal Parts
Lesson · about 35 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Last module the perfect tense followed a rule. This week it stops following the rule — so Latin gives you a cheat sheet instead.
For 1st and 2nd conjugation, you could predict the perfect: add -āv- or -u- to the stem. Third conjugation refuses to be predictable — its perfects twist the whole word (dūcō becomes dūxī, agō becomes ēgī). So every Latin verb is listed with four principal parts, and the third one hands you the perfect stem for free. Today there is nothing to conjugate — just one idea: learn to read a verb's four parts, and the dictionary has already done the hard work.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to name the four principal parts of a Latin verb and say what each one gives me.
2
I will be able to point to the third principal part as the source of the perfect stem.
⏰ PART 1 · WARM-UP 5 min

What you already do with the perfect

Last module you built perfects like these. Read them and remember the feeling of a completed action.

Cicerō laudāvit.

Cicero praised / has praised. (1st conj. perfect — predictable -āv- stem)

Mīlitēs timuērunt.

The soldiers were afraid / feared. (2nd conj. perfect — -u- stem; mīles = soldier)

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
You know 3rd conjugation verbs like dūcere (to lead) and agere (to do) in the present. What do you expect their perfect (“led,” “did”) to look like?
📚 PART 2 · THE BIG IDEA 12 min

Four parts, four jobs

From now on, learn every verb the way a dictionary lists it: with its four principal parts. Here is the model verb for this module, dūcō (to lead):

dūcō, dūcere, dūxī, ductum
PartFormWhat it gives you
1stdūcō“I lead” — the present-tense “I” form
2nddūcerethe infinitive — confirms the family (short -ere = 3rd conj.)
3rddūxī“I led / I have led” — the source of the perfect stem
4thductumused for forms you’ll meet later; for now, just recognize it

You already met parts 1 and 2 back in Module 10, when you learned the 3rd conjugation present. What’s new is part 3 — the perfect form. Look how far dūxī travels from dūcō: the c hardens into an x. You could never have guessed that. That’s the whole point.

💡 WHY LATIN BOTHERS

Because 3rd conjugation perfects can’t be computed, Latin simply tells you the answer up front. Learn the four parts once, and you never have to guess a perfect again — the third part is always waiting there for you.

✅ PART 3 · READ THE FOUR PARTS 15 min

Find the part that matters

No conjugating today — just train your eye to spot which of the four parts is which. Wrong answers give a hint; use Reset to try again.

✅ TRY IT · which part?

One out loud: the verb scrībō, scrībere, scrīpsī, scrīptum means “to write.” Which of the four parts would you use to build “he wrote” — and why? Say your reasoning, then reveal.

The third part, scrīpsī — because the perfect stem always comes from the third principal part. Tomorrow you’ll trim it to scrīps- and add an ending to make scrīpsit, “he wrote.”
