★ The Perfect Stem, Set Free
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Here is the good news you’ve earned: the hard part of the 3rd conjugation perfect is already done for you.
The unpredictable part — the perfect stem — is handed to you in the third principal part. Once you have it, the rest is a pattern you already know: the same six perfect endings you learned last module. Today you take the third part, drop the -ī, and build the whole tense: dūxī, dūxistī, dūxit… No new endings — just new stems.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to find a perfect stem by dropping -ī from the third principal part.
2
I will be able to add the six perfect endings -ī, -istī, -it, -imus, -istis, -ērunt to any 3rd conjugation verb.
3
I will be able to tell a present form from a perfect form (e.g. legit vs. lēgit).

📚 PART 2 · GET THE STEM 8 min

Step one: drop the -ī

Take the third principal part — the perfect “I” form — and drop the final -ī. What’s left is the perfect stem. This is exactly the move you made last module; only the stems are new.

3rd principal partDrop -īPerfect stem
dūxīdūxīdūx-
dīxīdīxīdīx-

Notice these stems look nothing like the present stems (dūc-, dīc-, ag-, scrīb-). That is why the third part had to be given to you — and why, once you have it, you’re home free.

✅ PART 3 · ADD THE ENDINGS 20 min

Step two: the six perfect endings

Add the six perfect endings to the stem. These are the same endings you used for 1st and 2nd conjugation last module — they never change. Here is the model verb dūcō (to lead), fully conjugated in the perfect:

1st persondūxī — I leddūximus — we led
2nd persondūxistī — you leddūxistis — you all led
3rd persondūxit — he/she/it leddūxērunt — they led

💡 ONE MACRON, TWO TENSES

Watch legō (to read): present legit means “he reads,” but perfect lēgit means “he read.” The only visible difference is the long ē in the perfect stem lēg-. Same with faciō: facit (he does) vs. fēcit (he did). The macron is doing real work — read it carefully.

Now put it to work — five reps, building forms and reading endings both directions. Keep the charts in view; the goal is to make the endings automatic. Answers vary, so read each one closely.

Your turn to build the whole thing: the perfect stem of mittō (to send) is mīs-. Say all six perfect forms out loud, singular then plural. Then reveal to check.

mīsī, mīsistī, mīsit, mīsimus, mīsistis, mīsērunt — “I sent, you sent, he/she sent, we sent, you all sent, they sent.” Same six endings as always; only the stem mīs- is new.
📌 PART 4 · ONE TENSE, TWO STEMS 6 min

Present vs. perfect, side by side

Every 3rd conjugation verb now lives in two places: a present built on the first part, and a perfect built on the third. Keep them straight and you can move a sentence through time.

Present — from part 1

dūcit — he leads
dīcit — he says
scrībit — he writes

Perfect — from part 3

dūxit — he led
dīxit — he said
scrīpsit — he wrote
