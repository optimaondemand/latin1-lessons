📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
This week the story starts to move — because this week your sentences learn how to do things.
So far every Latin sentence you can write uses esse (to be): Dīdō rēgīna est — “Dido is a queen.” True, but still. Today there is no paradigm to memorize and nothing to drill — just one big idea: Latin packs its action into verb families, and this module opens the first and largest one. By Friday you will be writing sentences where people sail, carry, sing, and fight. Today we just learn to recognize that family on sight.

📚 PART 2 · THE BIG IDEA 12 min

Being vs. doing

The verb esse only ever equates: it sets one thing equal to another. An action verb does something different — it lets a subject act, often upon an object.

Being — esse

Nauta est.
The sailor is / exists.

Links or states. Nothing is done to anything.

Doing — action verb

Nauta portat.
The sailor carries.

The subject performs an action — and can act on an object.

Latin has hundreds of action verbs, and it keeps them organized in four families called conjugations. Every verb in a family shares the same pattern of endings, so once you learn the pattern, you can use every verb in that family. This module opens Conjugation 1 — the largest and most regular of the four.

💡 WHY FAMILIES?

Think of conjugations the way you already think of declensions. You didn't memorize every noun separately — you learned the 1st-declension pattern once and applied it to nauta, terra, via… alike. Verbs work the same way: learn the family's pattern once, use it everywhere.

✅ PART 3 · SPOT THE FAMILY 15 min

The infinitive is the name tag

Every verb has an infinitive — its “to ___” dictionary form. In English: to love, to carry, to call. The infinitive is how you tell which family a verb belongs to. A first conjugation verb always ends its infinitive in -āre.

InfinitiveEndingMeaning
amāre-āreto love
portāre-āreto carry
vocāre-āreto call

If the infinitive ends in -āre, it is first conjugation. Other families end differently (you'll meet -ēre and -ere later) — so -āre is your reliable signal.

Work through all five — each one is a chance to train your eye on the -āre signal. Wrong answers give a hint; use Reset to try again.
