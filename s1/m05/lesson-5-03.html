Lesson 5.03
★ Eight Verbs — and the English Hidden Inside Them
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You have the pattern. Today you get the words — and discover you half-know them already.
This module's eight verbs are all first conjugation, so the paradigm from yesterday runs each of them. But here's the reward for learning Latin verbs: a huge share of English hides inside them. Learn portāre and you've explained portable, transport, import, export, and porter in one stroke. Today: meet the eight, and pull the English out of each.

📜 PART 3 · ETYMOLOGY MOMENT 6 min

One verb, a whole shelf of English

🌱 FROM LABŌRĀRE (TO WORK)

Labōrāre gives English labor directly. A laboratory is literally “a place where you work.” Collaborate is com- (with) + labōrāre — “to work together.” When a scientist says she is “laboring over” a result, she's using a word Roman farmers used for working their fields two thousand years ago.

This is why Latin vocabulary pays compound interest: each verb is a root that English has grown a dozen words from. You're not memorizing eight words today — you're unlocking scores of English ones.

✅ TRY IT · use the English clue
A pugilist is a boxer and pugnacious means eager to fight. So pugnāre means:

Now go the other way: habitat and inhabit come from habitāre. What does habitāre mean, and why does habitat fit? Say it, then reveal.

“To live in / to dwell.” A habitat is the place a creature lives; to inhabit is to live in. Both point straight back to habitāre — Latin roots turn English into a set of hints.
