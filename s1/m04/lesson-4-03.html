Lesson 4.03
★ Eight Nouns — and the English Hidden Inside Them
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
You have the endings. Today you get the words — and find you half-know them already.
This module's eight nouns are all second declension, so the paradigm from yesterday runs each of them. But here is the reward for learning Latin nouns: an enormous share of English hides inside them. Learn dominus (master) and you have explained dominate, dominion, dominant, and domain in one stroke. Today: meet the eight, and pull the English out of each.

📜 PART 3 · ETYMOLOGY MOMENT 6 min

One noun, a whole shelf of English

🌱 FROM DOMINUS (MASTER, LORD)

A dominus was the master of a Roman household — the one in charge. To dominate is to act like a dominus; a dominion is the territory a master rules; something dominant stands over the rest; your domain is the ground you command. Even the word dominus itself survives in the title clergy once carried, Dom, and in the phrase Anno Domini — “in the year of the Lord.”

🌱 FROM TEMPLUM (TEMPLE) — A HIDDEN STORY

A templum was originally not a building but a space — a section of sky an augur marked out to watch for omens in the flight of birds. To con-template (com- + templum) literally meant to gaze intently into that marked-off space. So when you contemplate an idea, you are doing what a Roman priest did with the heavens: looking hard into a bounded space to find meaning. And temple, of course, is templum almost unchanged.

This is why Latin vocabulary pays compound interest: each noun is a root English has grown many words from. You are not memorizing eight words today — you are unlocking scores of English ones.
