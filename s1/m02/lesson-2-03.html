📜 PART 3 · ETYMOLOGY MOMENT 6 min

The verb esse hides in plain sight

🌱 FROM ESSE (TO BE)

The verb for “being” naturally produced English words about what things are. The essence of a thing is what it truly is; something essential is part of that core being. Add a prefix and you get more: to be present is, at root, “to be before” (Latin prae- + esse), and its opposite, absent, is “to be away” (ab- + esse). Every time you mark someone present or absent, you're using esse.

This is why Latin vocabulary pays compound interest: even a tiny verb like esse is a root that English has grown a whole family of words from. You're not memorizing eight nouns today — you're unlocking scores of English words.
