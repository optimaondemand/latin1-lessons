Lesson 15.02
★ Ego, Tū, Nōs, Vōs
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday, the idea. Today, the words — and there are only a handful to learn.
The 1st- and 2nd-person pronouns come in just two shapes each: a subject (nominative) form and an object (accusative) form. Ego / mē = “I” / “me.” Tū / tē = “you” / “you.” In the plural, nōs = “we / us” and vōs = “you all,” each doing both jobs with one form. That's the whole set for the people who speak and the people spoken to.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to give the nominative and accusative of the 1st- and 2nd-person pronouns: ego/mē, tū/tē, nōs, vōs.
2
I will be able to choose the subject form or the object form correctly inside a sentence.

📚 PART 2 · THE FORMS 14 min

Two forms each — subject and object

Here is the full set for the 1st and 2nd persons. Notice how short it is. The nominative is the subject (the doer); the accusative is the object (the receiver).

PersonNominative (subject)Accusative (object)
1st singularego — Imē — me
2nd singulartū — youtē — you
1st pluralnōs — wenōs — us
2nd pluralvōs — you allvōs — you all
💡 TWO EASY PATTERNS

First: in the singular, the object form simply swaps the vowel — ego → mē, tū → tē. Second: in the plural, nōs and vōs don't change at all — one form covers both subject and object. Only four words to hold, and two of them do double duty.

See them at work. In each sentence, the nominative is the doer and the accusative is what the doing lands on:

Ego tē vocō.

I call you. — ego nom. (subject, emphatic) · tē acc. (object) · vocō 1st sg.

Tū mē vidēs.

You see me. — tū nom. (subject) · mē acc. (object) · vidēs 2nd sg.

Nōs vōs monēmus.

We warn you all. — nōs nom. · vōs acc. · monēmus 1st pl.

✅ PART 3 · PUT IT TO WORK 22 min

Pick the right form

Five reps — some build a form, some read one, some ask you to decide subject vs. object. Keep the table above in view; the goal is to make the choice automatic. Then write one of your own.

✅ TRY IT · object form
Which is the accusative (object) form meaning “me”?
✅ TRY IT · subject form
Which is the nominative (subject) form meaning “we”?
✅ TRY IT · translate
Translate: Tū mē vidēs.
✅ TRY IT · subject form
Which subject pronoun means “you all”?
✅ TRY IT · translate
Translate: Ego tē vocō.

Your turn to build. Write Latin for “We see you all” — use a subject pronoun and an object pronoun. Say your version, then reveal.

Nōs vōs vidēmus. — nōs (nom., we) + vōs (acc., you all) + vidēmus (1st pl. of vidēre). Since the verb ending already says “we,” the nōs here adds emphasis — but vōs as the object is genuinely needed.
