Lesson 16.04
★ The Curator's Voice — Planning Your Museum
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Commands and address aren't only for generals. They're the everyday voice of a guide welcoming you into a room.
Think of the last museum you walked through: Enter here. Look at this. Please don't touch. Every one of those is an imperative — and a good guide greets you by name. Today you read a Roman guide's voice, then turn to your own VR Aeneas Museum: what five rooms will you build, what belongs in each, and how will you address your visitor? You'll also trace this module's three verbs — imperāre, vocāre, nōlō — into a surprising amount of English.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to read and translate commands and direct address in a museum-guide context.
2
I will be able to plan five rooms for my VR Aeneas museum and connect imperāre, vocāre, and nōlō to English.
⏰ PART 1 · THE GUIDE'S VOICE 12 min

Reading a curator lead a visitor

Before you read, picture the tone. A museum guide isn't a general on a battlefield — yet a guide gives orders constantly.

💭 QUICK CHECK-IN · no wrong answer
Think of a guided tour. How many of the guide's lines are actually commands?

Now read each line aloud. A few words here are new — museum vocabulary — so they're glossed in the gray line. Watch for the imperatives (commands) and the vocatives (the visitor being addressed).

Salvē, hospes!

Greetings, guest! — salvē a greeting (literally the imperative “be well!”) · hospes guest, visitor (new; vocative = nominative, 3rd decl.)

Intrā et spectā!

Enter and look! — intrā command of intrāre, to enter (new) · et and · spectā command of spectāre, to look at, watch (new)

Hīc nāvem Aenēae vidēs.

Here you see the ship of Aeneas. — hīc here (new adverb) · nāvem acc. sg. of nāvis (ship) · Aenēae gen. (of Aeneas) · vidēs 2nd sg. (you see)

Lege titulum, amīce.

Read the label, friend. — lege command of legere (to read) · titulum acc. sg., label, title (new) · amīce voc. of amīcus (friend)

Nōlī tangere!

Do not touch! — nōlī + infinitive = negative command · tangere to touch (new; the classic museum sign)

💡 THE CURATING VERBS

Three easy first-conjugation verbs power a guided tour: intrāre (enter → entrance), spectāre (look at → spectator, spectacle), and mōnstrāre (show, point out → demonstrate). Their commands are regular: intrā / intrāte, spectā / spectāte, mōnstrā / mōnstrāte.

📚 PART 2 · VR MUSEUM WORKSHOP 14 min
🏛️ YOUR FIVE ROOMS · AENEAS, FROM TROY TO ROME (AENEID 1–6)

Back at the end of Quarter 1 you launched the VR Aeneas Museum project: a five-room virtual museum tracing Aeneas's journey from the fall of Troy to the shore of Italy. Today's job is to plan. A museum isn't a pile of objects — it's a path a visitor walks. Decide what belongs in each room, and how a guide would lead someone through it.

RoomLatin nameWhat belongs in it
1. The Fall of TroyTrōiaThe wooden horse, the burning city, Aeneas carrying his father Anchises out of the flames.
2. The Sea & the StormsMareThe Trojan fleet, Juno's storm, the anger of the gods, the long voyage west.
3. Carthage & DidoCarthāgōQueen Dido, the rising city, the doomed love, the fatal choice between amor and pietas.
4. The UnderworldĪnferīThe descent, the shade of Anchises, the parade of future Roman heroes.
5. Arrival in ItalyĪtaliaThe Trojans land; the future site of Rome; destiny fulfilled.

Each room needs a title, a short description, and at least one Latin caption — and this is where this week's grammar pays off. Address your visitor and direct them: Intrā, hospes! (“Enter, guest!”) · Spectā nāvem! (“Look at the ship!”) · Nōlī tangere! (“Do not touch!”). A vocative to welcome, an imperative to guide, a negative command to protect the exhibit.

🤵 COURTESY · THE ROMAN GUEST

Romans took hospitium — the sacred bond of host and guest — seriously. To welcome a stranger well was a mark of good character. When you address your museum visitor as hospes (“guest”) and invite them in, you are stepping into an old and honored Roman role: the gracious host who makes a visitor feel expected and safe.

📜 PART 3 · ETYMOLOGY MOMENT 6 min

Three verbs, a shelf of English

🌱 FROM IMPERĀRE (TO COMMAND)

The imperative mood is literally “the commanding” form. From the same root come imperious (bossy, commanding), and — through imperātor, “commander” — the English emperor and empire. A Roman general hailed imperātor by his troops had, in one word, both a military honor and the seed of a title that would rule the world.

🌱 FROM VOCĀRE (TO CALL)

The vocative case is the “calling” case — the case for calling someone by name. From vocāre English grows vocal, vocation (a “calling”), and a whole family of -voke words: evoke (call out), invoke (call upon), revoke (call back), convoke (call together).

🌱 FROM NŌLŌ (TO BE UNWILLING)

The nōlī of your negative commands is a form of nōlō. It survives in the phrase nōlēns volēns — “unwilling (or) willing,” that is, whether you like it or not. English still borrows it when something happens to a person nolens volens.
