Lesson 16.03
★ Calling by Name — and Saying “Don't”
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Before you order someone, you usually name them. And sometimes the order is: don't.
Today you finish the module's grammar with two pieces. First, the vocative — the case Latin uses to call someone by name (“Mārce!”, “fīlī!”). It looks just like the nominative for almost every noun, with one small family that changes. Second, the negative command: to forbid, Romans didn't just add “not” to an order — they said nōlī + the infinitive, literally “be unwilling to…”

⏰ PART 1 · WARM-UP 4 min

A greeting that's really a command

The everyday Roman “hello” was Salvē! (to one person) or Salvēte! (to several) — literally “be well!” It is a command form, an imperative.

📚 PART 2 · THE VOCATIVE CASE 13 min

The case of calling someone by name

The vocative is the case you use when you speak directly to someone. Good news: for almost every noun, the vocative is identical to the nominative. Only one group changes — second-declension nouns ending in -us or -ius.

NominativeRuleVocative“Hey, ___!”
Mārcus-us → -eMārceMarcus!
dominus-us → -edominemaster!
amīcus-us → -eamīcefriend!
fīlius-ius → -īfīlīson!
Cornēlius-ius → -īCornēlīCornelius!
rēgīnaotherwise = nom.rēgīnaqueen!
mīlesotherwise = nom.mīlessoldier!
💡 THE WHOLE RULE IN ONE LINE

If a noun ends in -us, swap it for -e. If it ends in -ius, swap it for a single -ī. Everything else — first declension, third declension, neuters, plurals — keeps the nominative form unchanged.

📌 PART 3 · SAYING “DON'T” 8 min

Negative commands with nōlī

To forbid, Latin does not simply add “not” to the imperative. Instead it uses the command form of the verb nōlō (“to be unwilling”) plus the infinitive:

To…FormulaExampleMeaning
one personnōlī + infinitiveNōlī timēre.Do not fear. (lit. “be unwilling to fear”)
a groupnōlīte + infinitiveNōlīte pugnāre.Do not fight. (to several)

Notice the shape: nōlī is itself a singular imperative (of nōlō), and nōlīte is its plural — the same -te you already know. The verb you're forbidding stays in its dictionary infinitive form: timēre, pugnāre, tangere.
