📜 PART 3 · ETYMOLOGY MOMENT 6 min

One verb, a whole shelf of English

🌱 FROM MONĒRE (TO WARN, ADVISE)

Monēre is the verb of the counselor and the watchman. A monitor is “one who warns” — and a screen earned the name because it keeps watch over what a machine is doing. To admonish is to warn firmly; a premonition is a warning felt before (prae-) the event. Even a monument is something built to remind — to warn the future not to forget. One Latin verb, and a whole vocabulary of memory and warning.

This is why Latin vocabulary pays compound interest: each verb is a root English has grown a dozen words from. You are not memorizing six words today — you are unlocking scores of English ones.

Now go the other way: habit, inhibit, and exhibit come from habēre. What does habēre mean, and why does exhibit fit? Say it, then reveal.

“To have / to hold.” To exhibit is to “hold out” something for others to see (ex- = out); to inhibit is to “hold in.” Even a habit is something you “have” and keep. All point back to habēre.
