📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
This quarter is about the power of the spoken word — and it opens by handing you a whole new family of verbs to speak with.
Welcome to Quarter 2. The theme ahead is rhetoric — the Roman art of persuasion, the skill that could turn an ordinary man into a leader of the Republic. Persuasion needs verbs of the mind: to see, to fear, to warn, to have. Those live in Latin's second conjugation. Back in Module 5 you learned the first family, tagged -āre. Today there is nothing to drill — just one idea: a second family exists, it wears the tag -ēre, and once you can spot it, the whole week opens up.

⏰ PART 1 · WARM-UP 5 min

One letter of difference

You already know the first conjugation by its tag: an infinitive ending in -āre, like amāre (to love) or portāre (to carry). Now look at three new verbs: vidēre (to see), habēre (to have), timēre (to fear).

📚 PART 2 · THE BIG IDEA 12 min

Latin has four verb families

Remember the plan from Module 5: Latin sorts every action verb into four conjugations, and each family shares one pattern of endings. You have already mastered Conjugation 1 (-āre). This module opens Conjugation 2, tagged with a long -ēre.

Family 1 — -āre

amāre — to love
portāre — to carry
vocāre — to call

Signature vowel: long ā.

Family 2 — -ēre

vidēre — to see
habēre — to have
timēre — to fear

Signature vowel: long ē.

The infinitive is still your name tag. If the vowel before -re is a long ē, the verb belongs to the second conjugation. That is the entire test.

💡 WHY A SECOND FAMILY MATTERS THIS QUARTER

Rhetoric is the art of moving an audience — and the verbs you need for it are second conjugation: a speaker makes the crowd vidēre (see) his point, timēre (fear) a danger, and monēre (heed a warning). You are learning the family the Roman orator lived in.
