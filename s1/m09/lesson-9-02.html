★ Same Six Endings, A New Stem
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Here is the good news: you already know most of today's lesson.
The second conjugation uses the exact same six personal endings you learned for the first conjugation — -ō, -s, -t, -mus, -tis, -nt. Only the stem vowel is different: a long ē instead of a long ā. Today you build the full present tense of vidēre (to see), and you learn the one small twist that sets the second family apart from the first.

📚 PART 2 · FIND THE STEM 7 min

Step one: get the stem

You get the present stem the very same way as before: take the infinitive and drop the -re. What stays behind is the stem — and this time it ends in a long ē.

InfinitiveDrop -rePresent stem
vidērevidērevidē-
habērehabērehabē-
monēremonēremonē-

That long ē is the signature sound of the family. Where the first conjugation kept its ā, the second keeps its ē.

✅ PART 3 · ADD THE ENDINGS 18 min

Step two: the six endings

Add the same six personal endings to the stem vidē-. Here is the model verb vidēre (to see), fully conjugated in the present tense:

SingularPlural
1st personvideō — I seevidēmus — we see
2nd personvidēs — you seevidētis — you all see
3rd personvidet — he/she/it seesvident — they see
💡 THE ONE DIFFERENCE FROM FIRST CONJUGATION

Watch the 1st person singular. In the first conjugation the stem vowel vanished: amā- → amō (the ā is gone). In the second conjugation the stem vowel survives: vidē- → videō — you still see the e. So amō but videō; portō but habeō. That kept vowel is your quickest way to tell the two families apart in a real sentence.

The endings themselves are identical to Module 5. Only the stem vowel changed:

Your turn to build a whole paradigm: recite the full present tense of timēre (to fear), all six forms, singular then plural. Say it out loud, then reveal to check.

timeō, timēs, timet, timēmus, timētis, timent — “I fear, you fear, he/she/it fears, we fear, you all fear, they fear.” Notice timeō keeps its e, just like videō and habeō.
