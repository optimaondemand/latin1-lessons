Lesson 11.01
★ The Tense That Looks Back
Lesson · about 35 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Every Latin verb you know speaks in the present. Today Latin gains a past.
So far your verbs only ever happen now: laudat — “he praises, he is praising.” But history is made of things that are already done. Today you meet the perfect tense: the tense of the completed past — laudāvit, “he praised, he has praised.” There is no paradigm to memorize today and nothing to drill — just one idea: a verb can point backward at an action that is finished. And that idea runs the whole quarter, because to praise a great Roman — the art of the encomium — you must tell what he did.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to explain what the perfect tense expresses — an action completed in the past.
2
I will be able to tell apart an action happening now (present) from one already finished (perfect).

📚 PART 2 · THE BIG IDEA 12 min

Now vs. done

A tense is the time a verb points to. Until now you have used only the present tense — action unfolding right now. The perfect tense points at an action that is already complete. Compare the same verb in both:

Present — happening now

Cicerō laudat.
Cicero praises / is praising.

The action is in progress as we speak.

Perfect — already finished

Cicerō laudāvit.
Cicero praised / has praised.

The action is over — a completed deed.

Notice the English perfect has two good translations: “praised” and “has praised.” Both describe an action that is done. Latin doesn't need a helper word like has or did — it builds the past straight into the verb, the way it already builds person and number into the ending.

💡 WHY “PERFECT”?

The name has nothing to do with being flawless. It comes from Latin perfectus, meaning “completed, finished, carried through.” A perfect-tense action is one that has been carried through to its end. Keep that meaning — it tells you exactly what the tense is for.

The first is present — the praising is happening now, ongoing. The second is perfect — the praising is a completed act, already carried through. Same people, same verb; only the time has changed, and Latin shows it by reshaping the verb into laudāvit.
