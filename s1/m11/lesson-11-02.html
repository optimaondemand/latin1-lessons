📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Here is the good news: the perfect tense uses one set of endings for every verb in Latin.
The present tense had different vowels for different families. The perfect is simpler: find the verb's perfect stem, then add the same six endings — -ī, -istī, -it, -imus, -istis, -ērunt — that work for all verbs, in every conjugation. Today you learn where the perfect stem comes from for 1st and 2nd conjugation verbs, and you build two full paradigms. Learn this pattern once and you can put any verb into the past.

📚 PART 2 · THE ONE ENDING SET 8 min

Six endings for every verb

In the present tense, each conjugation had its own vowel. The perfect drops all that: one set of personal endings serves every Latin verb, no matter its conjugation. Here they are — the six to memorize:

EndingPerson & numberEnglish subject
-ī1st singularI
-istī2nd singularyou
-it3rd singularhe / she / it
-imus1st pluralwe
-istis2nd pluralyou all
-ērunt3rd pluralthey

These are not the present endings (-ō, -s, -t, -mus, -tis, -nt). They belong to the perfect alone — and the moment you see -istī or -istis or -ērunt on a verb, you know at once you are looking at the completed past.

📌 PART 3 · FIND THE PERFECT STEM 8 min

Where the endings attach

The endings need a perfect stem to sit on. The perfect stem is different from the present stem, and how you build it depends on the conjugation.

1st conjugation — add -āv-

laudāre → stem laudāv-
portāre → portāv-
amāre → amāv-

Regular and predictable: nearly every -āre verb takes -āv-.

2nd conjugation — -u- or a vowel change

habēre → stem habu-
monēre → monu-
vidēre → vīd- (vowel change)

Most add -u-; a few change the vowel instead. These you learn per verb.
💡 HOW A DICTIONARY SHOWS IT

A Latin dictionary lists the perfect stem for you in the verb's third form: laudō, laudāre, laudāvī or habeō, habēre, habuī. That third form — the 1st person singular perfect — hands you the stem: just drop the -ī. Tomorrow you'll meet all of this module's verbs in exactly that format.
