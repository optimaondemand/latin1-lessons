You have the pattern. Today you get the words — each carrying its perfect stem, ready for the past.

📜 PART 3 · ETYMOLOGY MOMENT 6 min

The vocabulary of honor

🌱 FROM LAUDĀRE, GLŌRIA, HONOR

Laudāre gives English laud (to praise) and laudatory (full of praise); to applaud is to praise with clapping hands. Glōria becomes glory and the verb glorify. And the Latin noun honor is nearly unchanged in English honor, honorable, and honorary. Notice the pattern: the words a culture uses for recognition tend to survive for thousands of years — because every culture needs a way to say “this deed deserves praise.”

Even the grammar term is a praise-word in disguise: perfectus (“carried through, completed”) gives English perfect and perfection. The perfect tense, fittingly, is the tense that reports a deed brought to completion — exactly the kind of deed an encomium praises.
