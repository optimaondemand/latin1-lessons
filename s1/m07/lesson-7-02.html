★ The Dative Endings & the Four-Case Chart
Lesson · about 40 minutes
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Yesterday you learned what the dative does. Today it grows an ending — and it's a short one to learn.
The dative has only three forms to add to what you know: -ae for a 1st declension noun in the singular, -ō for a 2nd declension noun in the singular, and -īs for the plural of both. Today you slot those into the full four-case chart — nominative, genitive, dative, accusative — so you can see exactly where the new case lives.
🎯 BY THE END OF THIS LESSON YOU WILL BE ABLE TO…
1
I will be able to give the dative endings for 1st and 2nd declension, singular and plural.
2
I will be able to lay out the full four-case chart for a 1st and a 2nd declension noun.
3
I will be able to tell the dative apart from look-alike forms that share the same spelling.

📚 PART 2 · THREE ENDINGS 8 min

The whole dative in one small table

Here is every dative ending you need for 1st and 2nd declension. Notice how little there is to learn — and that the plural is identical for both declensions.

DeclensionDative singularDative plural
1st (e.g. via, nauta)-ae  (viae = to/for the road)-īs  (viīs)
2nd masc. (e.g. servus)-ō  (servō = to/for the slave)-īs  (servīs)
2nd neut. (e.g. dōnum)-ō  (dōnō)-īs  (dōnīs)
💡 THE SHARED PLURAL

The dative plural -īs is the same for 1st and 2nd declension. That's a gift: nautīs (to the sailors) and servīs (to the slaves) wear the exact same ending. One ending covers the plural everywhere you've learned so far.

⚠️ WATCH FOR LOOK-ALIKES

In 1st declension, viae can be genitive singular, dative singular, or nominative plural — they're spelled the same. The sentence tells you which: if the word is a receiver after a verb of giving or telling, it's dative. Context, not spelling, decides.

✅ TRY IT · the shared ending
Which ending marks the dative plural for both 1st and 2nd declension?

Your turn to produce a pair: give the dative singular and the dative plural of servus (slave). Say both, then reveal.

Dative singular: servō (to/for the slave). Dative plural: servīs (to/for the slaves). Singular takes 2nd declension -ō; plural takes the shared -īs.
