Case plus words equals reading. Today the dative carries meaning — and Aeneas walks among the dead.

📚 PART 2 · READ ALONG 12 min

Sentences with a receiver

Read each aloud. Find the subject (nominative), the thing acted on (accusative), and the receiver (dative). The gray line is your parse and translation — try it yourself before you read it.

Aenēās deō dōnum dōnat.

Aeneas gives a gift to the god. — Aenēās nom. (subject) · deō dat. sg. (receiver) · dōnum acc. sg. (thing given) · dōnat 3rd sg.

Sibylla Aenēae viam mōnstrat.

The Sibyl shows the way to Aeneas. — Sibylla nom. sg. (the prophetess, glossed) · Aenēae dat. sg. · viam acc. sg. · mōnstrat 3rd sg.

Umbra Aenēae fābulam narrat.

The shade tells Aeneas a story. — umbra nom. sg. · Aenēae dat. sg. (receiver) · fābulam acc. sg. · narrat 3rd sg.

Anchīsēs Aenēae umbrās mōnstrat.

Anchises shows the shades to Aeneas. — Anchīsēs nom. sg. (the father, glossed) · Aenēae dat. sg. · umbrās acc. pl. · mōnstrat 3rd sg.

Nautae deīs dōna dōnant.

The sailors give gifts to the gods. — nautae nom. pl. · deīs dat. pl. (receivers) · dōna acc. pl. · dōnant 3rd pl.

💡 READING TIP · THREE JOBS

With verbs of giving, telling, and showing, expect up to three players: a doer (nominative), a thing (accusative), and a receiver (dative). Latin word order is flexible — let the endings, not the position, tell you each word's job.

📚 PART 3 · CULTURAL READING 13 min
🏛️ AENEAS IN THE UNDERWORLD · AENEID, BOOK 6

Aeneas comes at last to Italy. There, at Cumae, a prophetess called the Sibyl guides him on the strangest leg of his journey: a descent, while still alive, into the land of the dead. To pass, he must first find and pluck a golden bough as a gift for the queen of the underworld. Carrying it, he goes down.

He crosses the black river Styx, ferried by grim old Charon; he passes Cerberus, the three-headed hound at the gate. In the mournful shadows he glimpses the shade of Dido — she turns away and will not speak. But Aeneas is searching for one soul above all: his father Anchises, who had died on the voyage.

He finds him in Elysium, the sunlit fields of the blessed. There Anchises does something remarkable: he shows his son the souls who are waiting to be born — a parade of future Roman heroes, including Romulus, the great generals, and Augustus himself. “Roman,” Anchises says in effect, “your art will be to rule the peoples and to crown peace with law.” Aeneas climbs back to the living world carrying not treasure but purpose: he has seen what Rome is for.

⚖️ ELYSIUM AND TARTARUS · A JUST AFTERLIFE

The Romans imagined the world below as sorted. Elysium was the region of light and rest, home to the virtuous and the dutiful. Tartarus was the deep pit of punishment, where the wicked paid for their crimes. To a Roman, the geography of the afterlife was a statement about justice: how you live — whether you honor gods, family, and country — decides where you belong.

Notice that Aeneas's reward in the Underworld is not comfort but a mission. This is the same pietas you met with Dido: duty carried forward, even beyond the grave.
