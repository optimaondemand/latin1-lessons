📜 PART 3 · ETYMOLOGY MOMENT 6 min

The “giving” root — and the case named after it

🌱 FROM DŌ, DŌNUM (TO GIVE, A GIFT)

The Latin root for giving stands behind a whole family of English words. A donation is a gift; a donor is a giver; to condone is (originally) to “give” a wrong a pass. Even pardon hides it — per- (thoroughly) + dōnāre (give), “to give completely,” that is, to forgive.

Best of all: the very name dative comes from Latin dātīvus, “pertaining to giving,” from dō (I give). The grammar term tells you what the case is for — it is the case of the one you give to.

This is why Latin vocabulary pays compound interest: learn one root and you unlock a shelf of English words — and sometimes the grammar label itself.

What does dōnum mean?

Now think it through: the grammar word dative comes straight from the Latin verb dō (“I give”). Why is that a fitting name for this case? Say your reasoning, then reveal.

Because the dative is the case of the one you give to — the receiver. A name built from dō (“I give”) points exactly at its job: it marks the person a gift, a message, or a showing is directed toward.
