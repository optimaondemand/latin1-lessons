⏰ PART 1 · THE WEEK IN ONE TABLE 5 min

Everything you built this week

DayWhat you learned
3.01A case is a noun's job; nominative (-a) = subject, accusative (-am) = object
3.02The four forms: -a, -am, -ae, -ās; the ending — not word order — carries the meaning
3.03Eight nouns: nauta, puella, fēmina, terra, via, aqua, silva, īnsula
3.04Reading real SVO sentences; Aeneas leaves Troy; Juno vs. Venus
✅ PART 2 · MIXED PRACTICE 20 min

Five mixed reps, drawing on the whole week — declining, parsing, and translating in one place. Then build a sentence of your own. (Action verbs are glossed, as they will be in the graded work.)

✅ TRY IT · decline
Make terra the plural subject (“the lands,” nominative pl.).
✅ TRY IT · parse
In Puellae aquam vident, what case and number is puellae?
✅ TRY IT · translate
Translate: Nauta viam videt. (videt = “sees”)
✅ TRY IT · decline
Make īnsula the plural object (“the islands,” accusative pl.).
✅ TRY IT · translate
Translate: Venus nautās amat. (amat = “loves”)

Write your own: make one full Latin sentence meaning “The sailors see the land.” Use a nominative plural subject, an accusative singular object, and the glossed verb vident (“they see”). Then reveal one correct version.

Nautae terram vident. — nautae (nom. pl. subject) + terram (acc. sg. object) + vident (“they see”). If you wrote Nauta terram vident or Nautae terra vident, check the endings: a plural subject needs -ae, and the object needs -am.
